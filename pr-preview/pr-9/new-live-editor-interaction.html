    Live editor interaction — the rules taken from the reference editor¶

    Status: measured, written down as acceptance criteria, and implemented where section 4 says so. This page records what the reference equation editor (iMathEQ) does with a click, a caret, a selection and a structure, and what that obliges our live math panel to do. Section 3 is the reference; section 4 is our side of it, with what is met and what is still missing.

    1. Why a reference¶

    The rendering engine is ours from scratch, but the interaction model is where a math editor is actually judged, and it is the part that is easiest to get subtly wrong — the panel shipped a palette that looked live and was inert, and caret movement that silently dropped keystrokes. iMathEQ was chosen as the reference because it is the model the reviewers asked for and it is freely inspectable.

    2. How the rules were measured¶

    The reference renders into a canvas (bg_canvas / fore_canvas) and reads the keyboard from a hidden textarea.efmase_focus_textarea. It exposes its state through window.editor, which is what made measurement possible at all:

    Call Gives
    window.editor.getMathML() the whole equation, as MathML
    window.editor.setMathML(xml) install an exact fixture, so every probe starts from a known tree
    window.editor.hasFocus() whether the editor holds the keyboard

    Each probe is then: install a fixture, perform one interaction, type a distinctive marker (9), and read the MathML back. Where the marker lands is the caret, so the caret needs no screen coordinates.

    Two artefacts invalidated the first rounds of probes, and both would have produced confident, wrong rules:

    • Synthetic mouse events carry offsetX/offsetY of 0. The editor reads those, so every scripted click looked like a click at the far left and reported "the caret is always at the start". They have to be defined onto the event (Object.defineProperty) before dispatch.
    • A second click inside the multi-click window selects a token. Probing faster than the library's click window turned a caret placement into a selection, and typing then replaced a token — which reads exactly like "the editor rewrites your equation when you click it". Probes are therefore spaced by at least 200 ms, which also produced the multi-click rules in section 3.

    3. The rules¶

    R1 — a click snaps to the boundary it is nearest¶

    Fixture abc, clicking at a range of horizontal offsets and typing 9:

    Click offset (px from the canvas's left edge) Result Caret
    2 … 26 9abc before a
    30 … 38 a9bc after a, before b
    40 … 48 ab9c after b
    52 and beyond abc9 after c

    The left half of a glyph is before it, the right half after it, and clicks well to the left of the first glyph clamp to the start. Acceptance: a click places the caret at the nearest character boundary; the halves of a glyph are distinguished.

    R2 — a click past the content appends at the top level¶

    Fixture x^2. Clicking at offset 60, 120 (baseline) or 40 px below, then typing +, gives

    <msup><mi>x</mi><mn>2</mn></msup><mo>+</mo>

    The operator is a sibling of the power, not content of its exponent. Clicking "after the formula" therefore means "after the last thing on the line", even when the line ends in a structure. Acceptance: the end of a line is a real position, and typing there appends to the line's own row.

    R3 — a click inside a slot targets that slot¶

    Fixture 1/2, typing 9 after a click: offset 34 at y = 8 … 22 lands in the numerator (<mfrac><mrow><mn>1</mn><mn>9</mn></mrow><mn>2</mn></mfrac>), at y = 45 in the denominator. Acceptance: the click's row decides the slot.

    R4 — a slot accepts a compound expression¶

    With the caret in the exponent of x^2, typing + then 1 gives

    <msup><mi>x</mi><mrow><mn>2</mn><mo>+</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow></msup>

    The same holds for a fraction's numerator, where typing + then 1 gives <mfrac><mrow><mo>+</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><mn>2</mn></mfrac>, and a denominator accepts multi-token content as <mfrac><mn>1</mn><mrow><mn>2</mn><mn>9</mn></mrow></mfrac>. Note what is absent: no bracket element, no (, no ). The slot's own wrapper (<mrow>) is the grouping, because the reference's interchange is structured MathML. Acceptance: any slot accepts any expression, including operators; entering an operator in a slot never blocks the next keystroke.

    R5 — an empty slot is an empty container, and survives¶

    The palette inserts a structure whose slots are <mrow/> — the same representation our engine uses for an unfilled slot. Backspace strips a slot to that state and then stops: a second and third backspace leave <mfrac><mn>1</mn><mrow/></mfrac> unchanged. The structure is not removed by emptying it. Acceptance: emptying a slot leaves a holding state; nothing is auto-deleted.

    R6 — palette insertion puts the caret in the first empty slot¶

    A palette press inserts the empty structure and places the caret inside it, so the next keystroke fills the first slot. Palette buttons respond to a pointer press sequence: a scripted .click() is ignored, so an automated test must send mousedown, mouseup and click.

    R7 — navigation is structural, and Tab is part of it¶

    From a fraction's numerator, ArrowDown types into the denominator and ArrowUp returns to the numerator; ArrowRight and Tab both leave the numerator for the denominator, and the marker lands before its content. Vertical movement keeps the horizontal position; horizontal movement and Tab walk the slots in reading order. Acceptance: Up/Down move between a structure's rows, Left/Right and Tab move to the next slot.

    R8 — selection replaces¶

    Dragging from one character boundary to another selects that range (abc → drag across bc), and typing replaces it outright (abc → a9). Multi-click is distinguished: a click inside the library's multi-click window selects the token under the pointer, and the next keystroke replaces it. Acceptance: a range can be selected and typing or Delete replaces it; a double-click has a different meaning from a single click.

    R9 — grouping is never spelled with brackets¶

    A linear chain exported from the reference stays a flat token row:

    <mi>x</mi><mn>2</mn><mo>+</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>+</mo><mn>3</mn>

    Structure appears only where a structure exists (msup, mfrac). This is a property of the reference's storage, and it is the reason R4 costs it nothing.

    4. What that obliged us to do¶

    Our storage is Calcpad text, not MathML, so R2 and R4 were not free: both were unmet, and both needed the same missing piece — a position, or a node, that the printer and the parser agree on.

    Rule Today Mechanism
    R1 met the caret snaps to the nearest boundary, the halves of a glyph are distinguished (nearestAnchor)
    R2 met a row ending in a structure has an explicit trailing position (trailingAnchor), the click past the content reaches it (withRowEnd), and insertion there nests what it follows the way the parser reads it (appendAtRowEnd)
    R3 met a click inside a slot lands on the leaf that was clicked
    R4 met an insertion that makes a slot compound gives it the group the printer already prints — mo('('), the parser's own reading of the content, mo(')') — so the text and the tree agree and the gate accepts it (bracketSlots)
    R5 met an unfilled slot is an empty container or an empty leaf, and isCommittableText refuses to write it into the document
    R6 met palette presses go through the field's own applyPalette, and the caret follows the new structure
    R7 met Up/Down are structural (moveVertical), Left/Right cross slots (moveHorizontal), and Tab/Shift+Tab walk the slots (slotAnchors, nextSlot) — at the last one the key is not intercepted, so focus leaves instead of the field trapping it (§4.3)
    R8 met a range is two anchors (orderedRange); drag, double click, triple click and Ctrl+A select it, and typing, Backspace and Delete replace it (deleteRange, replaceRange); the browser paints it (§4.3)
    R9 not applicable our interchange is linear text, so a group has to be spelled out — which is exactly what R4 now does

    Two mechanisms carry R2 and R4. An end-of-row anchor is a gap at children.length on a row whose last child is not a leaf; anchorRect places it at the previous sibling's right edge, and an insertion there groups what precedes the caret the way the parser does (a + b + 1 nests to mrow[mrow[a,+,b],+,1], 1 + 2*3 to mrow[1,+,mrow[2,*,3]]), because a flat splice is refused as a drift. Brackets as nodes: when an insertion makes a slot compound, the slot is given the group the printer was already printing, and the caret is re-based into it by counting anchors — the group adds four (( and ) contribute two each) and keeps the content's order and text, so the caret's ordinal survives the reparse that produced it.

    4.1 Two rules our storage adds, which the reference does not need¶

    Neither is a measurement of the reference; both follow from Calcpad text and were established against our own parser, so each is listed with the reading that forces it.

    An operand at the row end continues the last token. In a structured interchange, appending a digit after a group is a new token. In text, the appended text is all there is: a = x^2 then 1 prints a = x^21, and the parser reads that exponent as 21 — so mrow[msup[x,2], mn(1)] would be a tree its own printer disagrees with. The row end therefore descends to the last leaf for an operand (a = x^21, a = b^2c1, a = sqrt(92)) and stays at the top level for an operator, which is the R2 rule.

    An operator typed in a subscript belongs to the row outside it. A subscript is one token in the language: q_(1+2) parses as the name q_ beside a group, so there is no spelling for a compound subscript. Splitting the operator into the subscript left a tree whose printed text claimed the operator was at the top level — q_1 +, with the + actually inside msub. It now leaves the ownership and lands in the enclosing row: q_1 + 2, a = q_1 * 2, (q_1 + 2)/2, x^(q_1 + 2).

    4.2 Making the caret and the waiting slots visible¶

    Three defects made parts of an equation impossible to edit, and none of them was a rule:

    • An unfilled slot was a dead zone. Its click box was built by spreading a DOMRect, and a DOMRect keeps its fields on the prototype — so every coordinate was NaN, the candidate could never win, and clicking a slot placed the caret at the end of the line instead. The box is now copied field by field (boxOf).
    • The caret swallowed clicks. It is created imperatively, so it carried no scope id and the component's scoped rules never matched it: it stayed an in-flow transparent block over the maths, pointer-events: none and all. Its rules go through :deep now, which is also why it finally paints as a 1.5px cursor instead of nothing.
    • The row end borrowed the whole field's height, so it outscored every glyph for clicks that were not exactly on one and the caret jumped to the end of the line. Its band is the line's own boxes now.

    A slot waiting for a value is drawn as a dashed box (emptySlotPaths → .cp-math-slot), which is what makes R3 and R5 visible instead of a rule the editor knows and the user does not. Clicking the box targets that slot, because its box is a candidate again.

    4.3 Selection, and Tab as a slot walk¶

    Both remaining rules needed a range the tree decides, not the DOM.

    A selection is two anchors. orderedRange puts them in the caret's canonical order, so a drag that ends before it started is the same range, and rangeLength counts the characters between them. Deleting walks forward from the start with a budget: each live character removes exactly one, and an emptied leaf is dropped without spending budget. Deleting backward looked simpler and was wrong — the collapse of a slot takes the character beside it, so the loop stopped one short and typing after the delete merged into the leftover (q_1 replaced by 9 became 9q).

    A selection delete keeps the operator. Removing b^2 from sqrt(a^2 + b^2) leaves sqrt(a^2 + ), not sqrt(a^2): the place after + is where the replacement goes, and without the operator the next keystroke was refused as an unreadable row end. The same reason keeps = when the whole right side of a definition is deleted.

    Tab walks slots, then gets out of the way. slotAnchors collects the first position of every structural slot — a fraction's branches, a power's base and exponent, a matrix cell — entering a delimited group inside its bracket. Tab moves to the next, Shift+Tab to the previous, and at the last slot nextSlot returns null, the key is left alone and focus leaves the equation. The backwards walk steps out of the slot the caret is in, not to the nearest slot start behind it, or Shift+Tab in a denominator would land on that denominator's own start.

    The browser paints the range. It is handed to document.getSelection through domPointAt (the inverse of hitTest), so the engine draws the highlight and the caret is hidden while one is up. Drag (with a 3px threshold), double click (the token under the pointer, a scripted name whole), triple click (the expression) and Ctrl+A choose a range; Ctrl+C/X still act on the line, because the clipboard is Calcpad text.

    The browser run caught two defects the unit tests could not. A press called clearSelection, which also forgot the drag origin, so every drag collapsed to a click. And = was pruned like any dangling operator, so deleting a definition's right side left a, where no keystroke could rebuild it.

    5. Not verified¶

    Kept separate so that nothing here is mistaken for a measurement:

    • Tab beyond a structure — verified from a numerator to its denominator, back again, and out of the field at the last slot; a slot inside a delimited denominator is covered by unit tests only.
    • Deleting a whole structure — Backspace was tested to the empty slot only; how the reference removes a structure (Delete, the palette's "Back Space", or selecting it) was not established.
    • Undo granularity — the editor has Undo/Redo buttons (efmase_palettebtn_disabled when the stack is empty) but what constitutes one step was not probed.
    • Paste, and the multi-click token scope — a double click selected the token under the pointer, but which tokens count (a number, a whole name, a bracket pair) was not determined.
    • The palette's insertion mode. Pressing Superscript then typing 2, + produced a new msup per token (<mi>x</mi><mn>2</mn><msup><mo>+</mo><mrow/></msup>), which reads as a "superscript for the next token" mode. It was not reproduced under real input, and the effect is consistent with the synthetic-key artefacts in section 2, so it is not a rule here.
